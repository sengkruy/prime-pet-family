Cat care · 6 min
Guide 01 · Newborn to weaning

How to care for a baby kitten.

For very young kittens, warmth, their mother's milk, and steady growth matter more than toys or treats. Your job is usually to protect the nest and notice changes early.

Keep the nest warm, quiet and dry

Newborn kittens cannot regulate body temperature well. Give the mother a private, clean nesting area with bedding that stays dry and space for kittens to move away from a heat source.

Watch the pattern: nurse, sleep, grow

  • Healthy newborns spend most of their time nursing or sleeping.
  • Weigh each kitten daily during the first weeks and record the result.
  • Frequent crying, chilling, poor nursing, weakness, or weight loss needs veterinary attention.

If the mother cannot feed them

Use a commercial kitten milk replacer, not cow's milk, and get veterinary instruction on temperature, feeding position, frequency, and elimination support. Never feed a chilled kitten before warming it safely.

Try this today

Create a one-page kitten log with time, weight, nursing, urine, stool, and anything unusual. Patterns reveal problems faster than memory.

Where Prime fits

Prime solid foods are not for newborn or unweaned kittens. Introduce only when the kitten reaches the minimum age on pack and is developmentally ready, using veterinary guidance.

Cat care · 5 min
Guide 02 · Pregnancy and nursing

How to care for a mother cat.

A nursing mother, or queen, does most of the work. Give her privacy, easy access to essentials, and enough observation to spot when tired becomes unwell.

Build a low-stress care zone

Keep her nest away from noise, children, and other pets. Place food and water nearby, but keep the litter box separate. Change soiled bedding without constantly rearranging the nest.

Her appetite should work hard too

Pregnancy and lactation demand extra energy. Nursing cats often do best with energy-dense food suitable for growth, gestation, lactation, or all life stages, offered as several small meals. A veterinarian should guide portions and body condition.

Know the urgent signs

  • Not eating, extreme weakness, or ignoring the litter
  • Painful, hot, or hard mammary glands
  • Foul discharge, fever, tremors, panting, or poor coordination
  • Kittens crying persistently or failing to gain weight
Try this today

Put her food, water, and a simple observation notebook within easy reach of the nesting room, then give her space.

Where Prime fits

Some Prime Cat packs are labelled for all life stages, but that does not automatically confirm gestation or lactation suitability. Show the exact pack to your veterinarian before using it for a pregnant or nursing cat.

Nutrition · 7 min
Guide 03 · Mother and baby nutrition

What should mother and baby cats eat?

They share a family, not identical feeding needs. The mother needs dense nutrition for milk production; the kitten moves from milk to carefully introduced solid food.

For the nursing mother

Choose a complete food specifically appropriate for growth and reproduction, or one your veterinarian confirms for all life stages. Offer small, frequent meals and unlimited clean water. Her energy needs often rise as kittens grow.

For kittens

Mother's milk is the foundation early on. Kittens commonly begin investigating soft solid food around three to four weeks, but weaning is gradual. Use a complete kitten or growth food and follow veterinary advice for texture and schedule.

Keep their bowls separate

Separate measured bowls let you see who ate, protect the mother's higher-energy portion, and prevent a bold kitten from replacing a balanced plan with random snacking.

A simple weaning idea

Offer a shallow dish of age-appropriate kitten food softened to an easy texture. Let kittens explore, never push their faces into the food.

Where Prime fits

The supplied Prime Cat packs state minimum ages. Prime Classic and canned products shown begin from two months; they are not substitutes for mother's milk or kitten milk replacer. Introduce gradually only when age-appropriate.

Nutrition · 5 min
Guide 04 · Wet-food basics

Why can canned food be important?

Canned food is not automatically better than dry food, but its moisture, aroma, and texture make it a useful tool for hydration, variety, and appetite.

Water arrives inside the food

Canned cat food typically contains at least 75% moisture. That makes it a meaningful dietary source of water, especially for cats that do not visit the water bowl often.

Texture and aroma can invite eating

Many cats find moist food highly palatable. Different proteins and textures can help families discover what a selective cat accepts, but sudden changes can still upset the stomach.

Read the nutritional statement

Some cans are complete and balanced; others are complementary foods or toppers. The label determines whether a can can provide a full diet or should only form part of the bowl.

Open-can hygiene

Cover and refrigerate leftovers promptly, follow the manufacturer's use-after-opening guidance, and wash the bowl after each moist meal.

Meet Prime canned food

Dog care · 6 min
Guide 05 · First week home

Your new puppy's calm first week.

The goal is not to show your puppy the whole world in one day. Build safety first: a predictable routine, supervised exploration, and gentle positive experiences.

Start with one safe zone

Set up a bed or crate, water, appropriate chew toys, and an easy-clean area. Remove cables, medicines, toxic plants, and small objects. Supervise freedom until you know the puppy's habits.

Use a rhythm they can learn

  • Feed an age-appropriate growth diet on a consistent schedule.
  • Offer potty breaks after waking, eating, play, and before sleep.
  • Alternate short play and training with plenty of rest.
  • Arrange a veterinary visit to review health, parasites, and vaccinations.

Socialize without overwhelming

Introduce people, sounds, surfaces, and handling gradually, pairing novelty with calm rewards. Ask your veterinarian how to balance early socialization with local infectious-disease risk.

Try this today

Write tomorrow's simple schedule: wake, potty, meal, play, rest, and ask everyone in the home to follow the same cues.

Where Prime fits

The Prime Dog freeze-dried bag is labelled for puppies and adults. Use the puppy feeding table and ask your veterinarian to monitor growth, especially for large breeds.

Cat + dog care · 5 min
Guide 06 · Water every day

Hydration hides in the routine.

Water supports temperature control, digestion, circulation, and normal organ function. The easiest hydration plan is one your pet encounters naturally all day.

Make water easy to choose

Place multiple clean bowls in quiet, accessible locations. Keep cat water away from litter trays and prevent one pet from guarding the only bowl. Some cats prefer wide bowls or fountains.

Food contributes too

Wet food can increase a cat's total water intake. Dry-food eaters normally need to drink more from the bowl, so fresh water should always be available beside every Prime dry recipe.

Notice changes, not only empty bowls

Suddenly drinking far more or far less can matter. Lethargy, weakness, poor appetite, vomiting, diarrhea, tacky gums, or sunken eyes warrant veterinary advice.

Try this today

Wash every water bowl, refill it, and add one extra station somewhere your pet already likes to rest.

Where Prime fits

Prime canned food can contribute dietary moisture; Prime dry and freeze-dried foods should always be served with free access to fresh water.

Cat + dog care · 4 min
Guide 07 · Healthy shape

Look beyond the number on the scale.

Body Condition Score looks at fat stores; Muscle Condition Score looks at muscle. Together they reveal more than weight alone.

The at-home monthly check

  • From above: look for a visible waist behind the ribs.
  • From the side: look for an abdominal tuck appropriate to the animal.
  • With gentle hands: ribs should be easy to feel under a light covering, not sharply visible or buried.
  • Track weight and photos under similar conditions.

Why portions change

Age, neuter status, activity, climate, pregnancy, growth, and illness all influence energy needs. Package guides are starting points, not permanent prescriptions.

Try this today

Take one top-view and one side-view photo. Repeat monthly and bring the sequence to your next veterinary visit.

Where Prime fits

Use the feeding table on each Prime pack, then adjust the amount, not the nutritional balance, based on body condition and veterinary guidance.

Nutrition · 4 min
Guide 08 · Gentle food change

Change the bowl without surprising the stomach.

A gradual transition gives the digestive system and the pet's preferences time to adapt. Seven days works for many healthy pets; sensitive animals may need longer.

A simple seven-day path

  • Days 1-2: 25% new food, 75% old food
  • Days 3-4: 50% new food, 50% old food
  • Days 5-6: 75% new food, 25% old food
  • Day 7 onward: 100% new food if stools and appetite remain normal

Slow down when the pet tells you

If mild stool changes appear, return to the last well-tolerated ratio. Repeated vomiting, significant diarrhea, pain, marked lethargy, or a cat refusing food needs veterinary advice, not endless recipe switching.

Try this today

Pre-measure both foods for the first three days. It is much easier to transition consistently when nobody is guessing at the bowl.

Where Prime fits

Prime packaging includes a gentle transition guide. Use it every time you move to a new Prime recipe, even when the brand stays the same.

Nutrition · 6 min
Guide 09 · Smarter shopping

How to read a pet-food label.

The front tells the story. The back tells you whether the food fits your pet. Five label areas do most of the useful work.

1. Species and life stage

Confirm cat or dog, then check kitten, puppy, adult, senior, gestation, lactation, or all-life-stages suitability.

2. Nutritional adequacy

Look for a statement explaining whether the food is complete and balanced, and for which life stage. A topper or complementary food should not silently replace a complete diet.

3. Ingredients and analysis

Ingredients appear in descending order by weight. Guaranteed analysis gives minimum or maximum levels for nutrients such as protein, fat, fibre, and moisture, but it does not measure ingredient quality by itself.

4. Calories and feeding guide

Calories help compare portions between wet and dry products. Feeding tables are starting points; monitor body condition.

5. Company and storage information

Keep the lot code, best-before date, and manufacturer contact details. Follow resealing, refrigeration, and storage directions.

Try this today

Photograph the full label before discarding a bag or can. If your pet reacts badly, you will still have the recipe, lot, and contact information.

Cat knowledge · 5 min
Guide 10 · Breed and pattern basics

General knowledge about Calico cats.

The first thing to know is simple: calico is not one single breed. It is a coat pattern, usually showing white with large orange-red and black patches, and it can appear across many different cat breeds and mixed-breed cats.

What makes a cat a calico?

Most people picture the classic tri-colour look: white, black, and orange-red. Some calicos are dilute, which means the darker shades look softer, more like blue-grey and cream. The pattern itself does not tell you whether the cat is long-haired, short-haired, large, small, quiet, playful, pedigreed, or mixed.

Why are most calicos female?

The coat pattern is linked to sex chromosomes, which is why calico cats are overwhelmingly female. Rare male calicos do exist, but they are unusual and often have reproductive differences. For everyday pet owners, this is mostly an interesting fact, not a reason to expect a different personality.

Personality depends on the individual cat

People often say calicos have a strong attitude, but temperament is shaped much more by the individual cat, early handling, environment, and breed background than by colour pattern alone. Some calicos are bold and talkative; others are gentle, quiet, or shy.

Care needs are the same as other cats

  • Feed for life stage, body condition, and health needs, not for coat pattern.
  • Brush according to coat length, especially if the cat has medium or long fur.
  • Monitor weight, appetite, litter-box habits, and hydration like any other cat.
  • Choose nutrition based on age, routine, and veterinary guidance.
Try this today

If you live with a calico cat, take one clear photo in natural light and keep a simple record of her coat, body condition, and weight over time. It is an easy way to notice change early.

Where Prime fits

Prime chooses food by life stage, feeding format, and household routine, not by coat pattern. A calico kitten, adult, or senior still needs the same careful food matching as any other cat.

Dog knowledge · 6 min
Guide 11 · Breed basics

General knowledge about dog breeds.

Dog breed names can tell you something useful, but not everything. A breed gives clues about likely size, coat type, energy level, and the kind of work a dog was originally developed to do. It does not guarantee the exact personality, training level, or health of one individual dog.

Why breeds exist

People developed different dog breeds for different jobs: guarding, herding, hunting, retrieving, companionship, scent work, and more. That history still matters today. A dog bred for long active work often needs more exercise and structure than a dog bred mainly for companionship.

What to look at before choosing a breed

  • Size: think about space, transport, lifting, and how much food a larger dog may need.
  • Energy: some breeds want long walks, games, training, or jobs every day.
  • Coat care: grooming needs can range from easy weekly brushing to frequent maintenance.
  • Temperament: friendliness, alertness, independence, and trainability vary widely.

Breed group helps, but lifestyle matters more

Sporting, hound, working, terrier, toy, non-sporting, and herding groups can help families start their research, but the real question is always the same: does this dog fit your home, routine, budget, and experience?

Purebred and mixed-breed dogs both need thoughtful care

Purebred dogs may be more predictable in size and type, while mixed-breed dogs can bring a wider blend of traits. Either way, the best approach is the same: learn the likely needs, ask honest questions, train patiently, and choose food according to age, body condition, activity, and veterinary advice.

Try this today

Before falling in love with a look, write a short list of what your household really needs: size, energy, grooming, noise tolerance, and time for training. That usually leads to a better dog match.

Where Prime fits

Prime Dog food is chosen by life stage, routine, appetite, and daily activity, not by breed name alone. A small calm companion and an active larger dog may need very different portions even if they eat from the same product line.

Cat breed · 5 min
Guide 12 · Breed spotlight

General knowledge about Siamese cats.

Siamese cats are known for their slim build, colour-point coat, strong voice, and very social nature. They often want more conversation, more interaction, and more daily stimulation than people expect from a cat.

They usually like company and activity

Many Siamese cats are affectionate, curious, and vocal. They often follow people from room to room and can become bored when the home environment stays too quiet or empty for long periods.

Build routine and enrichment into the day

  • Offer climbing space, window views, and regular play sessions.
  • Use puzzle feeding or treat hunts to keep the brain busy.
  • Give them calm social time, not only independent toys.
  • Watch body condition because active cats still overeat when portions drift upward.

What families often love most

The Siamese personality can feel very companion-like. Many households enjoy their expressiveness, intelligence, and willingness to stay involved in family life.

Try this today

Schedule two short play sessions at the same time each day. Predictable interaction usually helps social cats settle and complain less.

Where Prime fits

Siamese cats do not need a special breed-only recipe. Choose Prime by life stage, appetite, and feeding format, then keep portions steady enough to protect a lean body shape.

Cat breed · 6 min
Guide 13 · Breed spotlight

General knowledge about Persian cats.

Persian cats are loved for their plush coat, calm expression, and quieter way of living. Their beauty is real, but so is the everyday grooming work that keeps the coat and skin comfortable.

Coat care is part of routine care

Long dense fur can mat if brushing is inconsistent. Many Persian families do best with gentle daily combing, routine eye-area cleaning, and a calm handling routine from an early age.

Low-drama homes often suit them well

Many Persians enjoy peaceful indoor routines more than constant noise or rough play. That does not mean they want to be ignored; it means they often prefer gentle interaction and stable habits.

Keep the food plan simple and clean

  • Offer fresh water in easy-to-reach places.
  • Keep bowls clean so facial fur stays tidier around meals.
  • Monitor stool quality, coat condition, and weight over time.
  • Choose food by life stage and health needs, not coat appearance alone.
Try this today

Set out a fine comb, a soft cloth, and a weekly coat-check reminder. Persian care feels much easier when the routine stays small and regular.

Where Prime fits

Prime can support a Persian household through dry, freeze-dried, or canned formats depending on preference and hydration goals. The important match is life stage, body condition, and what the cat comfortably eats.

Dog breed · 6 min
Guide 14 · Breed spotlight

General knowledge about Golden Retrievers.

Golden Retrievers are friendly, trainable, and family-oriented, but they are not low-effort dogs. Their best life usually includes movement, grooming, social time, and a job to do.

Friendly does not mean self-managing

Goldens often fit family life beautifully, yet they still need consistent manners, recall practice, leash training, and calm boundaries around excitement.

Exercise and coat care matter every week

  • Plan daily walks plus play, sniffing, or training games.
  • Brush regularly because the double coat sheds year-round.
  • Watch calories carefully when treats increase during training.
  • Use food portions that match activity, not just body size.

They usually do best with people nearby

Golden Retrievers are social dogs. They often thrive in homes where someone enjoys shared activity, gentle training, and steady companionship.

Try this today

Swap one random treat session for a short training walk. Goldens often look their best when exercise and learning happen together.

Where Prime fits

Prime Dog freeze-dried food can suit active family dogs when portions are adjusted for body condition and exercise. Larger active breeds especially benefit from regular weight and growth monitoring.

Dog breed · 5 min
Guide 15 · Breed spotlight

General knowledge about Pomeranians.

Pomeranians are tiny dogs with a much bigger personality than their size suggests. They can be bright, bold, vocal, and charming, but their small frame means handling and safety matter every day.

Small body, big awareness

Because they are lightweight dogs, rough handling, high jumps, and uncontrolled interactions with larger dogs can create problems quickly. Home setup matters just as much as temperament.

What helps a Pom thrive

  • Short daily walks and play rather than only being carried.
  • Regular coat brushing to manage the dense double coat.
  • Gentle training for barking, leash manners, and confidence.
  • Portion control because tiny dogs can gain weight fast.

Companion dogs still need structure

Pomeranians often love family closeness, but they still benefit from predictable meal times, calm greetings, and clear routines that prevent attention-seeking habits from taking over the home.

Try this today

Check that your Pom can step safely onto beds or sofas without launching off high surfaces. Little joints appreciate thoughtful setup.

Where Prime fits

A Pomeranian may eat only a small amount each day, so consistency matters. Measure Prime portions carefully and use the feeding table as a starting point, then fine-tune with your veterinarian.

Cat care · 5 min
Guide 16 · Indoor enrichment

How to keep an indoor cat active and interested.

Indoor cats can live beautifully indoors, but the environment must work harder for them. Movement, climbing, hunting-style play, and quiet safe zones all help prevent boredom and stress.

Think in three layers: floor, furniture, and height

Many indoor cats become more confident when they can walk, hide, scratch, climb, and watch the room from above. Vertical space matters almost as much as floor space.

Use food as enrichment, not only a bowl

  • Try puzzle feeders or small food hunts.
  • Rotate toys instead of leaving every toy out all the time.
  • Play in short sessions that end with a calm rest period.
  • In multi-cat homes, give each cat space and its own resources.

Watch for boredom signs

Restlessness, overgrooming, scratching in unwanted places, or rough play with people can all suggest that the home needs more outlets for normal cat behaviour.

Try this today

Add one new climbing, scratching, or hiding option near a window. A small change in layout can create much more movement during the day.

Where Prime fits

Prime dry food can work well in measured puzzle feeders, while canned food can add variety and moisture to the daily routine. Indoor cats often benefit from planned portions rather than unlimited refills.

Senior cat care · 6 min
Guide 17 · Older-cat nutrition

How to feed a senior cat more thoughtfully.

Older cats do not all need the same bowl. Some gain weight, some lose it, some drink less than ideal, and some want to eat but struggle with comfort, smell, or routine. Senior feeding works best when you watch the cat in front of you.

Watch weight, muscle, and appetite together

Ageing can change body composition before families notice it. A senior cat may look fluffy while losing muscle, or become thinner because illness, dental discomfort, or poor appetite is creeping in.

Make meals easier to accept

  • Offer measured meals instead of leaving food out all day.
  • Use shallow bowls in comfortable, easy-to-reach places.
  • Support hydration with fresh water and, when appropriate, wet food.
  • Ask a veterinarian before changing to a senior-labelled recipe.

Small appetite changes matter more in older cats

If a senior cat eats much less, loses weight, or seems interested in food but cannot eat normally, treat that as useful health information and contact a veterinarian promptly.

Try this today

Weigh your senior cat, take a side photo, and note how easily they finish a measured meal. That tiny record becomes very valuable over time.

Where Prime fits

Prime canned food can help some older cats with aroma, texture, and moisture, while dry or freeze-dried formats may still suit cats with good appetite and comfort. The right choice depends on life stage suitability, body condition, and veterinary advice.

Dog nutrition · 5 min
Guide 18 · Appetite and routine

What to do when a dog is a picky eater.

Some dogs are genuinely selective. Others become inconsistent because the routine is inconsistent. Before changing foods again and again, steady the feeding plan and pay attention to what the dog is telling you.

Start with structure, not panic

Offer meals on a schedule, keep the bowl down for a limited time, and reduce extras that teach the dog to wait for something tastier. A predictable rhythm gives you clearer information.

What helps many picky dogs

  • Feed in a calm place with a clean bowl.
  • Limit treats and table scraps between meals.
  • Make recipe changes gradually, not every day.
  • Track appetite changes instead of guessing from memory.

Know when it stops being ordinary fussiness

Sudden appetite loss, vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, or refusing food for more than a short period deserves veterinary attention, especially in puppies, seniors, or dogs with existing health issues.

Try this today

Pick one meal schedule and keep it for a full week before deciding whether the food itself is the real problem.

Where Prime fits

Prime Dog freeze-dried food can appeal to some selective dogs because of aroma and texture, but it should still be introduced gradually and measured carefully. The goal is a reliable complete diet, not a daily negotiation.
